Chapter 17: Borrowed fur

Six hours later, I'm still correcting the drift.

Right. Pause. Right again. The starboard engine pushes us crooked, and every few minutes I nudge the joystick to set things straight. A ritual now. Meditative.

Or it would be, if I didn't have a gnawing suspicion that my co-pilot turned off the heating entirely. My breath fogs. My fingers are stiff around the joystick, and my knuckles ache when I flex them.

The cabin thermostat is somewhere behind the wall above the bunk, tucked into a panel Ollie's had his paws in more than once. Wouldn't put it past him to have rewired something in the name of saving power and accidentally turned the Calyx into a refrigerator.

Despite his earlier offer to lend my own jackets back to me, they're currently serving as mattress and pillow for a sleeping Expie. And I don't have the heart to wake him early. Every time I shiver I picture him there, radiating heat into fabric that should be on my back.

At least the shivering kept me awake. The radar's been clean all shift. Not a single contact. Deep space doing what deep space does best: nothing at all.

Whatever. My shift's up. The nav display shows our heading at a clean twelve o'clock. We're maintaining our distance from the corridor. All's good.

Time to catch some sleep before Solen calls.

I grab the crutch and haul myself up. The ankle protests, a wet crunch that sends a tremor up my calf. Hard to know if my craving for fent is withdrawal or just my body begging for the pain to end.

I shuffle toward the cabin, driving the crutch down harder than necessary. Each impact rings through the deck plates—fair warning that the rightful owner of the bunk is inbound and expects it vacated.

Thunk. Thunk. Thunk.

The cabin is dark. The constellations are off for the first time in years; another power-saving measure. The noodle cup of grass sits on the workbench where Ollie left it, its blades curling further inward. Beside it, his hoodie hangs from the edge of the wire basket, damp, the yellow stain faded to a pale smudge.

Ollie's on the bunk, of course. He's pulled out the spare doona from the storage compartment beneath the frame. It covers him from the waist down in a puff of faded blue. Above it, he's a tight crescent of black fur, arms tucked inward, head buried against his chest as if to muffle the outside world. The very tip of his orange tail peeks from the edge of the doona.

He's too cute like this. But I'm too tired to care.

"You're on shift, Ollie."

Nothing. Not even a twitch. His breathing doesn't change. His ear-things don't move.

That's how I know he's faking it. A sleeping Ollie twitches. This Ollie is a statue, suspiciously still. He wants to make me feel bad for prodding him awake. He wants me to sigh and limp back to the cockpit out of guilt.

Nice try.

I lean the crutch against the bunk frame and ready my index finger. I jab—gently—at his side, right where the fur is thinnest over his ribs. "Wake up, stinky."

A muffled sound from inside the ball of fur. "I'm not stinky…"

Knew it. He can't resist a personal affront to his scent. "Up you get."

"Nooooo." He curls tighter, arms locking around his knees. "Five more minutes."

Nuh-uh. I'm tired. I'm sore. And worst of all: "I'm bloody freezing."

He doesn't respond at first. Perhaps weighing up whether he can still trick me into thinking he's asleep.

Then his muzzle shifts. A few degrees away from his chest. A sliver of orange appears between his lashes—the faintest flicker giving me the side-eye.

"Then borrow my fur."

"You aren't sharing."

"I am." His fur bristles, every strand lifting and standing on end. He looks like a black dandelion. "I'm always sharing."

He's not getting up. If anything, his arms have tightened around that furry ball he's turned himself into. Another shiver makes my legs tremble. A shot of pain as the contents of my ankle grind together like sandpaper.

Still not getting up? Fine. "Suit yourself, you fluffy bastard."

I grab the edge of the doona and lift it. The crutch clatters against the frame as I lurch sideways and slam down onto the bunk. My weight hits the thin mattress—and the springs send a shockwave through Ollie.

He squeaks.

His body bounces, floating above the mattress for one brief moment before hurtling back to earth. Before he can recover, I scooch beneath the doona and push him toward the wall.

This bunk was never built for two.

His body tenses, legs pulling tighter, spine going rigid. His fur brushes over my forearms, so soft it barely registers as contact at first—just silk that ghosts across my skin.

But beneath the softness, heat. My god, is he warm.

It hardly registers as a thought; my arms move on their own. They slide through his fur, around the curve of his back, my cold fingers pressing inwards until they find the warmest spot—his chest, where his chin meets the dense ruff of fur at his throat. The heat pools there like the idle exhaust of an engine. My fingertips sink in and the cold evaporates.

He's small against me. So small that my arms easily lap around him. His ribs rise and fall beneath my palms, each breath pushing warmth into my wrists. His ear-things brush the underside of my chin, the texture a bizarre mix of feather and horn. The doona settles over us both. Beneath it, his tail uncurls from his feet and rests against my shin.

He says nothing for so long. Neither do I. He simply relents to my touch. And I let every inch of his warmth envelop me. The cold drains from my fingers, and for the first time in hours, the shivering thins, stutters, and dies.

But a tremble beneath my palms—from Ollie, not me—snaps me back. It's faint. His breathing hasn't changed. His body hasn't pulled away. But the tremble is there, and it isn't from the cold.

"S-shit, uh…"

My fingers freeze against his chest. What am I doing? Knowing not even half of the hell he's been through—what other hands did to him— I shoved him against the wall just to warm my fingers in his fur. "I'm sorry. I… I should stop."

"No." His response is instant. "This is… nice, I think."

"Oh." A rush of relief. My hands sink back into his fur. "This is kinda nice for me too."

We settle back into each other. Fur against skin. His breathing steadies, and so does mine. His body softens a fraction more, his head tilting back until his ear-things brush the base of my throat.

We're both silent. There's a hesitancy in the stillness. It's the careful way his bandaged paw rests near my wrist without quite touching it. The way I keep my breathing shallow so as not to press too hard against his spine. The way he's a foot too small for a proper spoon.

But strangely enough, it's the legs that are most awkward. Digitigrade. Those extra joints. I can't quite figure out how to wrap mine around his—or even where his legs end and feet begin. And I'm pretty sure one of those 'legs' my toes keep stroking is actually the base of his tail.

But I don't care how awkward we are—how different our bodies are. Because fuck me, how badly I needed this. It's more than just a hug. It makes me feel like I'm somewhere else… somewhere with a windowsill.

I want to hold him like this forever.

But I can't. "One of us should really be flying the ship."

"Five more minutes."

Five minutes? Flying blind in unmapped space for five minutes? That's just reckless.

"Ten minutes," I say. "No more, no less."

Ollie sighs. "Fine…"

The fluff of his scruff tickles my nose. And then it hits me; now's the perfect opportunity. Nowhere suspect—just the back of his neck. One, quick, sniff.

I breathe him in.

Familiar fragments. There's burning dust, an oil heater being used for the first time. But then there's grass, the sort of thing that should burn, yet it lives. And last, faint, but so very familiar: a little bit of me.

"This is going to make me sound like such a loser," I say, talking into his fur. "But… haven't had many guests on board. Before you, that is."

"Really?" He sounds genuinely surprised. He's also talking into his own fur, as if it's a closed channel just for us. "How many? Before me?"

"Zero."

Being a courier is a lonely job. Somewhere between the first delivery and the thousandth, you stop noticing that the cabin only has one mug. And then you lose the second plate.

I expect him to scoff.

"That's not so bad," he says. "I mean, I've only borrowed fur with one other."

"Who?"

"The one in my cell." His chest tightens beneath my palm. "My friend."

They were the pair who survived. The pair the company couldn't let go. "The one you…"

"Yeah." He nods; even with my eyes covered by fur, I feel the motion. "The one I left behind. At the Spindle."

There it is. That's what they called it. The facility where they experimented on Ollie. On countless other Expies. A name the company buried so deep that even Solen's assistant doesn't know it exists.

"I miss him," says Ollie. "So much."

I hug him tighter. Just enough that he feels it. My arms pressing in. My chest firm against his back. He's not alone anymore.

Ollie's paw finds my wrist. His pads are soft enough to mould around my hand. His fingers thread through mine, and his claws settle softly against my knuckles.

We lie there in silence for what might be the longest ten minutes of our lives. I can't find anything else to say. Maybe nothing is worth saying.

Almost nothing. "We're in this together, Ollie."

"I know." His tail glides along my leg, curls between my thighs, wraps around my back. "That's why we're going back."

I lift my head from his fur. "Going back?"

"To Lindenfield. Then to Torren Relay." His head uncurls from his chest, and the orange of his eyes—two bright circles in the dark—look directly into mine. "Back to the Spindle."

The hull groans. Calyx limps through the void on one engine, dragging us back to where we should be running from.

"We're going to save him."
